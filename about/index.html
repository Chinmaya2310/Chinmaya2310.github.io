How this works

Generic Bachat is a small side project by Chinmaya Nayak, an M.Tech student at IIIT Allahabad. It doesn't sell anything and isn't connected to PMBI or the government.

Where the data comes from

Jan Aushadhi prices: PMBI's product list on janaushadhi.gov.in, downloaded on 17 November 2026. 2,453 products.

Kendras: PMBI's store list from the same site. 21,837 Kendras, with addresses, PIN codes and map locations.

Brand prices: the open Indian Medicine Dataset (MIT licence), collected from online pharmacy listings. It's a few years old, so brand prices are a guide, not a quote.

How a brand is matched

For each Jan Aushadhi tablet or capsule, I split the name into salts and strengths, for example “Pantoprazole 40 mg”. The same is done for every brand. Names are cleaned up so that “Amoxycillin” matches “Amoxicillin” and “Metformin Hydrochloride” matches “Metformin”, and strengths are converted to the same unit.

A brand only counts as a match if every salt and every strength is the same, and the form is the same (tablet or capsule). Sustained-release versions are only matched with sustained-release versions. Prices are compared per tablet, so different pack sizes are fair.

That gives 974 Jan Aushadhi medicines matched to 106,784 brands.

What it can get wrong

The brand dataset only records two ingredients per medicine. So a brand with three ingredients could look like a two-ingredient one. Zerodol-SP is the example that caught me: it has serratiopeptidase on top of the two painkillers. To guard against this, two-ingredient brands are dropped when their name has a marker that usually means a third ingredient (SP, Trio, Gold, AMH and a few others), or when the same company sells a shorter-named brand with the same listed ingredients. That catches most cases, but not necessarily all, which is why every result tells you exactly what your strip should say.

Syrups, injections, creams and devices aren't compared yet (1,479 Jan Aushadhi products), because their pack sizes are hard to compare fairly.

Found a wrong match? Email me at chinmayanayak018@gmail.com and I'll fix it.

Same salt and strength means the medicines should work the same way, but please check with your doctor or pharmacist first. Thyroid, epilepsy and heart-rhythm medicines in particular may need a check-up after you change brands.

This site only compares prices. It isn't medical advice, and it isn't connected to PMBI or the Government of India. Talk to your doctor or pharmacist before you switch any medicine.

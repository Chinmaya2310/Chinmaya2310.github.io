    Jan Aushadhi's full price list is public, but it's sorted by salt name, while people know their medicines by brand name. So most of us never find out that the strip we buy every month has a version that costs a fraction of the price. This site joins the two lists together.

    Questions

    What is Jan Aushadhi?

    A Government of India scheme, run by PMBI, that sells unbranded generic medicines through Jan Aushadhi Kendras. The medicines contain the same salts as the big brands and usually cost much less.

    Is the generic as good as the brand?

    In India a generic with the same salt, strength and form as a brand is meant to work the same way, and PMBI has every batch tested. Your doctor or pharmacist can tell you if switching is fine in your case.

    Why is the price at the Kendra different?

    Jan Aushadhi prices here are from PMBI's own list. Brand prices come from an older public dataset, so a brand's MRP today may be different. The price printed on the strip is the one that counts.

    My medicine isn't listed. Why?

    Either Jan Aushadhi doesn't make that exact salt and strength, or it's a syrup, injection or cream, which I haven't added yet. Only tablets and capsules are compared for now.

    Same salt and strength means the medicines should work the same way, but please check with your doctor or pharmacist first. Thyroid, epilepsy and heart-rhythm medicines in particular may need a check-up after you change brands.
